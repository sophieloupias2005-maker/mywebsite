Embedded Systems & Robotics Project: Mobile Robot

Toulouse INP-ENSEEIHT | Department of Electronics, Electrical Energy & Automation (EEEA) 2025-2026

Designed, configured, and programmed a 4-wheel drive mobile robot based on an Altera Cyclone V FPGA board (DE1-SoC) running an embedded 32-bit Softcore Nios II processor. Developed both hardware-level address decoding and software architecture in C across polling, periodic interrupt, and asynchronous event-driven paradigms.

Hardware Architecture & Memory Mapping: Designed and implemented full memory address decoding for custom peripheral interfaces over a 64-byte I/O space split into 8 distinct zones. Integrated hardware memory-mapped registers for 7-segment displays, LED status indicators, switches, DC motor controllers, optical line tracking sensors, and ultrasonic distance sensors. Verified memory access cycles, control signals, and chip-select timing using a digital storage oscilloscope.

Low-Level Firmware & Control Algorithms (C / Nios II HAL): Implemented full manual joystick control with directional motor driving algorithms (PWM-like pulse control) and optical line-following logic in polling mode. Built multi-tasking execution pipelines using dual hardware timers (TIMER_0 and TIMER_1) with preemption control to enforce task priorities. Designed edge detectors using D flip-flops in VHDL/Quartus for asynchronous joystick input and ultrasonic obstacle threshold triggers (< 20 cm).

Autonomous Navigation & Safety Integration: Programmed real-time active safety loops using 3 ultrasonic distance sensors (Center, Left, Right) to trigger immediate stopping or reverse overrides when obstacles fall within critical thresholds. Developed state-machine routines for line-tracking navigation and follower/slave vehicle modes.

Tools & Technologies Used: Altera Cyclone V (DE1-SoC Board), Nios II Softcore Microprocessor (32-bit), VHDL, Quartus Prime 17.1, Nios II Eclipse IDE, C programming language, Ultrasonic Sensors (SRF05), Optical Trackers, H-Bridge DC Motor Controllers, 7-Segment Displays, and Oscilloscope.

Maximum Power Point Tracking (MPPT) Algorithm

Travaux d'Initiative Personnelle Encadrés (Interdisciplinary Personal Initiative Project) 2024-2025

As part of the entrance exams for French engineering schools, we were required to research and solve a technical problem on the topic of environmental transition, energy conversion, and transformation.

Alongside two classmates, I chose to tackle optimization in solar energy—specifically how to maximize the power output of photovoltaic solar panels. We developed a Maximum Power Point Tracking (MPPT) algorithm that dynamically adjusts operating points despite variable external factors such as temperature, solar irradiance, and panel orientation. We then tested our algorithm by creating a real life model using Matlab and Solidworks. Once the model was tested and the algorithm was validated, we implemented the algorithm into an arduino UNO card. We connected our circuit to our solar pannel to test the MPPT algorithm in real life under real conditions.

Solar Charging Station & MPPT PCB Project

Power Electronics & Hardware System Design (In Progress)

Building upon my previous research on Maximum Power Point Tracking (MPPT), I am currently designing an autonomous solar charging station tailored for mobile phones and small electronic appliances.

To validate system behavior under dynamic real-world conditions, I first modeled the entire energy conversion chain in MATLAB/Simulink. The model integrates a photovoltaic solar panel, an optimized MPPT algorithm, a DC-DC buck converter, protective circuitry, and a battery stage emulating the load of the target device.

Following complete numerical simulation and accurate component dimensioning, the next phase involves transferring the schematic to KiCad to design and layout a custom Printed Circuit Board (PCB). Through this hands-on project, I am strengthening my competencies in circuit design, PCB routing, power conversion, and physical prototype assembly before performing final experimental tests with an active solar panel.
